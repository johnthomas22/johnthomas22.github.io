Atmospheric Physics & "Saturation Myth" Analysis

This simulator models Radiative Transfer and Outgoing Longwave Radiation (OLR)—the thermal infrared energy emitted by Earth's surface and atmosphere back into space.

Flaws in the "CO₂ Saturation" Claim

Arguments claiming CO₂ is "99% saturated" relies on simplified assumptions that ignore 3D atmospheric dynamics:

  • The Altitude Effect: Earth's atmosphere is not a single thin sheet of glass. Thermal radiation is absorbed and re-emitted thousands of times on its way upward. Radiation only escapes to space from the Effective Radiating Altitude, where air is thin enough for infrared rays to escape without hitting another CO₂ molecule. Adding CO₂ pushes this escape layer to higher, colder altitudes. Because colder air radiates less energy, Earth retains extra heat until the surface warms up.
  • Spectral Wings: While the center of CO₂'s absorption band (~15 µm) is nearly saturated near the surface, the "wings" (edges) of the absorption spectrum are not. Increasing CO₂ widens the range of wavelengths that get trapped.
  • Logarithmic Forcing: Radiative forcing scales logarithmically: $\Delta F = 5.35 \cdot \ln(C / C_0)$. Every doubling of CO₂ adds ~3.7 W/m² of trapped energy, producing continuous surface warming rather than zero impact.
  • Water Vapor Feedback: Water vapor is the dominant greenhouse gas, but it acts as a feedback mechanism. Warmer air holds more moisture (~7% per 1°C), doubling the warming initiated by CO₂.

Core Physics Summary

Concept Common Misconception Scientific Reality
CO₂ Saturation "CO₂ is saturated, so more emissions do nothing." Saturation only applies to ground-level absorption centers. Radiation escapes higher up in colder, unsaturated layers.
Diminishing Returns "Logarithmic growth makes added CO₂ negligible." Logarithmic growth means every doubling yields a steady ~2.5°C–4°C of surface warming.
Water Vapor "Water vapor renders CO₂ irrelevant." Water vapor amplifies CO₂ forcing by roughly 2x via atmospheric moisture feedback loops.

Definitions

  • OLR (Outgoing Longwave Radiation): Thermal infrared energy escaping Earth into space. At equilibrium, OLR equals net incoming solar energy (~239 W/m²).
  • Lapse Rate: The rate at which atmospheric temperature drops with altitude (~6.5°C per kilometer in the troposphere).
