Proof 1: Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS) Closed-Form Limit

At thermal equilibrium, time derivatives vanish (dΔTs/dt = 0, dΔTd/dt = 0). Consequently, the mixed layer and deep ocean equilibrate to the exact same temperature (ΔTs = ΔTd = ΔTeq). Substituting this into the mixed-layer ordinary differential equation:

Cm · (dΔTs / dt) = ΔF - λ·ΔTs - γ·(ΔTs - ΔTd) = 0
ΔF - λ·ΔTeq - γ·(ΔTeq - ΔTeq) = 0
ΔF - λ·ΔTeq = 0 ⟹ ΔTeq = ΔF / λ

☀️ Fundamental Physics: Why CO₂ Traps Outgoing Heat but Ignores Incoming Sunlight
A common point of confusion is why greenhouse gases do not simply absorb heat from the Sun as sunlight travels down through the atmosphere. The reason is the profound difference in wavelength between solar radiation and terrestrial heat (governed by Planck's Law and Wien's Displacement Law, λpeak ∝ 1 / T):
  • Inbound Solar Radiation (Shortwave Spectrum, ~0.2 to 4 µm): The Sun's surface is blisteringly hot (~5,778 K / ~5,500 °C). The atmosphere is transparent to incoming solar radiation across this entire spectrum because the linear carbon dioxide molecule (O=C=O) has electronic transitions only in the far ultraviolet and vibrational modes in the thermal infrared; it has zero absorption bands across the solar shortwave spectrum. 1. The Physical Foundation: Why Temperature Scales Linearly with Cumulative Emissions (TCRE)

In complex Earth System Models and observational records alike, global mean surface temperature rise exhibits a near-perfect linear proportionality to cumulative CO₂ emissions. This linearity is the result of a remarkable physical compensation between two opposing non-linear geophysical processes:

Simultaneously, upper-ocean stratification reduces downward heat diffusion into the deep abyss, leaving a larger fraction of trapped energy at the surface.

5. Net Zero, The Zero Emissions Commitment (ZEC), & Drawdown Rebound

A common misconception in climate discussions is: "If emissions drop to zero, will temperatures continue to rise for centuries?"

The physical answer, verified by the IPCC AR6 and the international Zero Emissions Commitment Model Intercomparison Project (ZECMIP, MacDougall et al. 2020), is no:

  • The Zero Emissions Commitment (ZEC ≈ 0.0 °C): When anthropogenic CO₂ emissions cease entirely (Net Zero), ocean heat uptake continues absorbing excess energy from the lower atmosphere. At the same time, the ocean and terrestrial biosphere continue drawing down atmospheric CO₂, causing atmospheric radiative forcing to decline. In physical climate models, these two opposing curves almost exactly offset: global surface warming essentially halts within a few years of reaching true zero emissions.
  • The Carbon Drawdown "Sink Rebound" Effect: While stopping emissions halts further warming, restoring atmospheric CO₂ back down to pre-industrial (278 ppm) or safe historic levels (350 ppm) requires active Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDR).
  • However, as atmospheric CO₂ partial pressure drops, the oceans and land biosphere begin outgassing a fraction of the carbon they previously absorbed. Consequently, removing 1.0 ppm from the atmosphere requires scrubbing not just 7.82 Gt CO₂, but approximately ~17 Gt of CO₂ from the active Earth system to overcome the outgassing rebound.

3. The 10:1 Trophic Calorie Inefficiency & Carbon Opportunity Cost of Livestock

While biogenic methane (CH₄) from enteric fermentation is an acute short-term warming driver, an even larger systemic climate impact of livestock agriculture is its thermodynamic calorie loss and disproportionate land-use footprint:

• Thermodynamic Calorie Loss (Lindeman's Trophic Rule): Animals lose ~90% of ingested plant calories to basal metabolism, body heat, and inedible tissue. Converting plant feed into animal meat requires 10 to 25 kg of plant feed per 1 kg of beef (~3 to 4 kg for pork and poultry).
• The 80% / 18% Land Disparity: According to Poore & Nemecek (2018, Science), livestock rearing (grazing land plus animal feed croplands) monopolizes ~80% of all agricultural land on Earth (~40 million km²), yet delivers only ~18% of global human calories and 37% of global protein. The remaining 82% of human calories are grown on just ~20% of farmland.
• 75% Land Reduction Potential: If human calories were consumed directly from plants, global agricultural land demand would shrink by up to 75%, freeing approximately 30 million km² (an area the size of the entire African continent).
• Carbon Opportunity Cost: Monopolizing 40 million km² in pasture and feed crops actively prevents natural forest and grassland regeneration. Allowing this land to naturally rewild would sequester an estimated 300 to 500+ Gt CO₂ in vegetative and soil biomass over several decades (Searchinger et al. 2018, Nature; Hayek et al. 2021, Nature Sustainability)—equivalent to nearly a decade of all global fossil fuel emissions combined.

5. Atmospheric Physics & The Methane Problem: The 20-Year Climate Brake

Methane (CH₄) occupies a critical, double-edged position in atmospheric physics. Often described as climate change's "emergency brake", methane operates under fundamentally different radiative, chemical, and temporal dynamics than carbon dioxide:

• 20-Year Potency (GWP₂₀ = 82.5 to 84×): Over a 20-year timescale, 1 tonne of CH₄ traps ~84 times more heat than 1 tonne of CO₂. Under the standard 100-year accounting metric (GWP₁₀₀ = 28 to 30×), methane's acute short-term heating is heavily diluted by mathematical averaging.
• Present-Day Warming Contribution: Methane is responsible for approximately +0.5 °C of forced surface warming (likely range 0.3–0.8 °C; IPCC AR6), often quoted as roughly 30% of the rise since the Industrial Revolution (the exact percentage depends on baseline and aerosol accounting).
• Atmospheric Lifetime (11.8 Years): Unlike CO₂ (which lingers for hundreds to thousands of years), CH₄ is actively destroyed in the troposphere via chemical oxidation by hydroxyl radicals (•OH).
• Ground-Level Ozone Formation: Atmospheric CH₄ oxidation in the presence of nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) produces toxic tropospheric ozone (O₃), causing an estimated ~255,000 premature deaths, ~775,000 asthma-related hospital visits, and ~26 million tonnes of lost staple-crop yield every year (UNEP/CCAC Global Methane Assessment, 2021).
A. Radiative Physics: Why Each Molecule Packs More Punch

Why is a molecule of methane so much more radiatively potent than a molecule of CO₂?

  • Band Saturation: Carbon dioxide's main absorption band (centered at 15 µm) is already largely saturated at ~427 ppm, so extra CO₂ acts mainly at the band edges (logarithmic forcing, ΔF ∝ ln(C/C₀)). Methane's fundamental band is centered at 7.66 µm (1,306 cm⁻¹) and, because CH₄ is present at only ~1.9 ppm, that band is far from saturated: forcing grows roughly with the square root of concentration (ΔF ∝ √C), so each added molecule still removes a lot of outgoing heat. (The band partly overlaps water-vapour and N₂O absorption, which trims its net effect.)
  • High Radiative Efficiency: Each additional CH₄ molecule exerts a radiative efficiency of ~0.00036 W/m² per ppb—about 26 times greater per molecule than CO₂ (~0.000014 W/m²/ppb; IPCC AR5/AR6). Per kilogram the advantage is smaller (~84× over 20 years) because CH₄ is lighter than CO₂ and is destroyed within ~12 years.
B. Chemical Destruction: The Hydroxyl Radical (•OH) Sink

Methane's concentration in the air is governed by a dynamic chemical equilibrium between surface emission and chemical destruction in the air:

CH₄ + •OH ⟶ •CH₃ + H₂O ⟶ ... ⟶ HCHO ⟶ CO ⟶ CO₂ + 2 H₂O

Roughly 90% of atmospheric methane is removed by reaction with short-lived hydroxyl radicals (•OH)—often called the atmosphere's "detergent"—in the sunlit troposphere. This gives methane an e-folding turnover lifetime of ~11.8 years. Consequently:

  • Rapid Climate Responsiveness: If global methane emissions are reduced today, atmospheric methane concentrations begin dropping within a few years. Cutting human-caused methane emissions by ~45% this decade could avoid roughly 0.3 °C of warming by the 2040s (UNEP/CCAC Global Methane Assessment, 2021).
  • The "Brake" vs. "Steering Wheel" Dilemma: Methane acts like a heater that turns off 12 years after emissions stop; CO₂ acts like permanent insulation. Cutting methane buys humanity crucial breathing room to prevent crossing catastrophic tipping points (such as permafrost collapse and marine ice-sheet loss), but methane reductions cannot substitute for CO₂ phaseout. If CO₂ emissions continue, cumulative atmospheric carbon will inevitably push global temperatures past safe thresholds once the methane reduction dividend is exhausted.

Page 8: The Clean Energy & Storage Transition: Replacing 41 Gt/yr Fossil Energy

Eliminating ~41 Gt/yr of global fossil CO₂ emissions requires deploying a clean energy system capable of delivering reliable power, heat, transport, and industrial feedstocks 24/7/365.

1. The Electrification Efficiency Dividend

A common misconception is that the world must replace all 135,000 TWh of fossil primary energy with 135,000 TWh of clean power. This overlooks the massive thermodynamic waste inherent to combustion engines and thermal power generation:

• Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) Vehicles: Convert only 20% to 30% of fuel energy into forward motion; ~75% is rejected as heat through radiators and exhaust pipes.
• Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs): Convert 85% to 90% of electrical energy into wheel motion (~3× to 4× less energy needed per kilometre).
• Gas & Oil Boilers: Deliver 80% to 90% thermal efficiency.
• Electric Heat Pumps: Draw ambient environmental heat with a Coefficient of Performance (COP) of 3.0 to 4.0 (delivering 300% to 400% heat per unit of power consumed, cutting energy demand by ~65% to 75%).
• Thermal Power Plants (Coal/Gas): Reject 50% to 65% of chemical energy into cooling towers and rivers.

The Bottom Line: Because electrified devices waste far less energy, fully replacing 135,000 TWh of fossil primary energy requires only ~70,000 to ~80,000 TWh/yr of clean useful electricity (we adopt 75,000 TWh/yr as the central planetary benchmark).

Addendum: Present-Day Forced Warming (+1.26 °C) vs. Headlines Reporting +1.5 °C Breached

A common point of inquiry when reviewing climate models is: "Why does this physical simulator model a warming anomaly of approximately +1.26 °C for 2026, when international headlines in 2023–2024 widely reported that global temperatures had already breached +1.5 °C?"

This apparent divergence is grounded in the critical distinction between single-year weather variability (driven by chaotic internal ocean oscillations like El Niño) and the underlying multi-decadal forced climate response formally defined by the IPCC and the United Nations Paris Agreement.

1. Single-Year Anomaly Spikes vs. The Multi-Decadal Forced Trend

During calendar years 2023 and 2024, observational monitoring agencies (Copernicus / ECMWF ERA5, Berkeley Earth, NOAA GlobalTemp, NASA GISTEMP) reported that global surface air temperatures temporarily reached +1.48 °C to +1.55 °C above the 1850–1900 reference level.

However, these single-year records were amplified by a temporary confluence of natural internal variability stacked on top of human global warming:

  • A Powerful El Niño (ENSO): El Niño events temporarily vent vast quantities of ocean heat from the tropical Pacific into the troposphere, spiking global mean surface temperatures by +0.10 °C to +0.25 °C above the forced baseline. As El Niño decays into neutral or La Niña conditions, global temperatures naturally drop back toward the forced trend line.
  • Solar Cycle 25 Maximum: The 11-year solar cycle reached its peak in 2024–2025, temporarily adding ~0.05 W/m² of top-of-atmosphere solar irradiance.
  • Marine Aerosol Reductions & Volcanic Forcing: New international shipping fuel rules (IMO 2020) reduced sulfur emissions, temporarily thinning reflective marine stratocumulus clouds over shipping corridors, while the massive January 2022 eruption of the Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai submarine volcano injected unprecedented water vapor directly into the stratosphere.

2. The Official UNFCCC & IPCC Definition of "Breaching 1.5 °C"

Under the Paris Agreement, the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), and IPCC AR6, a long-term climate threshold is not considered "breached" by a single unusually warm year or month. Climate is scientifically defined as the 20-year to 30-year running average of global temperatures, which filters out short-term ENSO noise:

• Single-Year Observational Spikes (2023–2024 El Niño Peak): +1.48 °C to +1.55 °C (relative to 1850–1900; temporary weather record)
• Human-Induced Forced Warming (Forster et al. 2024, ESSD): +1.31 °C [1.1 to 1.7 °C] in 2023, ~1.36 °C in 2024 (relative to 1850–1900)
• Simulator Model State (2026 Forced Anomaly): +1.26 °C rel. to 1750 (+1.14 °C rel. to 1850–1900)
• Projected Permanent Crossing of the 20-Year Mean (+1.5 °C): Early-to-mid 2030s (under continued emissions)

The IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6 Working Group I) and the authoritative Indicators of Global Climate Change 2023 (Forster et al. 2024, ESSD) define human-induced warming as the multi-decadal forced response to greenhouse gases and aerosols relative to the 1850–1900 reference period, estimated at +1.31 °C [1.1 to 1.7 °C] in 2023 and reaching approximately +1.36 °C in 2024. Relative to the unforced pre-industrial 1750 baseline (which includes ~0.12 °C of early warming between 1750 and 1900), the simulator's deterministic forced response reaches +1.26 °C in 2026 (equivalent to +1.14 °C above the 1850–1900 mean).

3. Pre-Industrial Baseline Differences: 1750 vs. 1850–1900

Another source of numerical variation in the literature is the choice of pre-industrial baseline:

  • The 1850–1900 Meteorological Baseline: Observational weather datasets (HadCRUT5, Berkeley Earth) use 1850–1900 because that was the earliest era with standardized, globally distributed thermometer networks.
  • The 1750 Pre-Industrial Baseline: In physical radiative transfer models (and Antarctic ice cores), the true pre-industrial baseline is 1750 CE, when atmospheric CO₂ was in steady-state equilibrium at 278 ppm prior to industrial coal combustion.

Between 1750 and 1880, early industrial coal burning and land-clearing had already elevated CO₂ from 278 ppm to ~290 ppm, producing approximately +0.12 °C of early warming before global thermometer networks were established. Consequently, tipping-point thresholds (Armstrong McKay et al. 2022) and Paris Agreement targets are referenced to 1850–1900, while physical radiative balance simulations are referenced to the 1750 unforced baseline. The simulator accounts for this by computing the model's 1850–1900 hindcast offset (mean +0.12 °C) and adjusting threshold crossings accordingly.

4. Why the Two-Layer Ocean EBM Models the Forced Signal

This simulator models the foundational physics of planetary energy balance and the Two-Layer Ocean Energy Balance Model (Held et al. 2010; Geoffroy et al. 2013; IPCC AR6 Chapter 7). Because it computes the deterministic thermal response to greenhouse gas forcing without synthesizing stochastic weather noise (like individual El Niño cycles or volcanic eruptions), it calculates the pure forced thermodynamic climate response.

At 427 ppm CO₂ in 2026, the model computes an Earth Energy Imbalance of ≈ +0.78 W/m² being absorbed by the ocean thermal sink (observational satellite & Argo ocean heat content estimates typically report ~0.8 to 1.0+ W/m² due to internal weather variability and aerosol changes), and the model's transient surface temperature anomaly lands precisely at +1.26 °C above the 1750 baseline (+1.14 °C above 1850–1900).
