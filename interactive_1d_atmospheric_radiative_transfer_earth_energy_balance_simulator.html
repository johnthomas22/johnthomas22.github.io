Atmospheric Radiative Transfer & Saturation Refutation Guide

This simulator models the vertical transfer of thermal infrared radiation through Earth's atmosphere, coupled with ocean heat uptake and climate feedback loops.

1. The "CO₂ Saturation Myth" Refuted

Online articles (such as those from the Heartland Institute) frequently claim that adding more $\text{CO}_2$ has "virtually no impact" because the atmosphere is already "99% saturated." This assertion stems from an oversimplified 1900-era laboratory misconception that ignores the three-dimensional physics of the atmosphere:

  • The Altitude Escape Layer: Earth's atmosphere is not a single, thin sheet of glass. Thermal infrared photons are absorbed and re-emitted thousands of times as they migrate upward. Radiation only escapes to space from the Effective Radiating Altitude ($z_{\text{eff}}$), where the air becomes thin enough that escaping photons have a clear path to space. Adding greenhouse gases makes the lower atmosphere more opaque, forcing this escape layer higher into colder air. Because colder air radiates less energy ($\sigma T^4$), outbound radiation to space drops, trapping excess heat.
  • Spectral Wings: While the exact center of $\text{CO}_2$'s $15\,\mu\text{m}$ absorption band is heavily saturated near the surface, the outer edges ("wings") of the spectral line are not. Increasing $\text{CO}_2$ concentration expands absorption across these wing wavelengths, capturing thermal radiation that previously escaped freely.
  • Logarithmic Forcing Does Not Mean Negligible Warming: Radiative forcing scales logarithmically: $\Delta F = 5.35 \cdot \ln(C / C_0)$. While each additional ppm has a slightly smaller marginal effect than the last, every doubling of concentration adds approximately $3.7\text{ W/m}^2$ of trapped energy, resulting in continuous, steady surface warming.
  • Water Vapor is an Amplifier (Feedback), Not an Alternative: Water vapor is the most abundant greenhouse gas, but its concentration is governed by temperature (Clausius-Clapeyron relation: $\sim 7\%$ more moisture per $1^\circ\text{C}$ of warming). $\text{CO}_2$ is the non-condensing control knob that drives initial warming, which then pulls more water vapor into the air and doubles the net temperature response.

2. Earth Energy Imbalance (EEI) & The Transient Stage

Outbound radiation to space does not remain constant:

  • Stage A (Equilibrium): Incoming absorbed solar radiation ($\text{ASR} \approx 239.0\text{ W/m}^2$) matches Outgoing Longwave Radiation ($\text{OLR} = 239.0\text{ W/m}^2$). The net energy balance is $0.0\text{ W/m}^2$.
  • Stage B (Forcing Shock / Active Emissions): When $\text{CO}_2$ rises, the radiating layer moves higher into colder air. Outbound OLR drops (e.g., to $235.3\text{ W/m}^2$). The difference ($239.0 - 235.3 = +3.7\text{ W/m}^2$) is the Earth Energy Imbalance (EEI). This surplus trapped heat warms the planet, with over 90% accumulating in the global ocean.
  • Stage C (Restoration of Balance): As the surface and atmosphere warm, they radiate more heat. OLR slowly rises back toward $239.0\text{ W/m}^2$. Once OLR reaches $239.0\text{ W/m}^2$, the imbalance drops back to zero, but Earth is left at a permanently hotter equilibrium temperature.

3. Continuous Emissions & Persistent Imbalance

Because human emissions have climbed by $+2.0\text{ to }+2.5\text{ ppm/yr}$ over recent decades, the climate system is pushed continuously away from equilibrium. Because the massive heat capacity of the ocean takes decades to catch up with atmospheric forcing, $\text{OLR}$ is kept persistently lower than $\text{ASR}$, sustaining the modern satellite-observed energy surplus of approximately $+0.8\text{ to }+1.2\text{ W/m}^2$. Reaching Net Zero emissions stabilizes greenhouse concentrations, allowing the planetary energy imbalance to slowly decay toward zero as committed warming finishes playing out.

4. Model Verification & Calibration (Two-Layer Ocean EBM)

To prevent the unphysical runaway warming typical of simple optical depth approximations (which erroneously produced $\approx +2.93^\circ\text{C}$ at only $357\text{ ppm}$), this simulator implements the peer-reviewed, IPCC AR6-calibrated Two-Layer Ocean Energy Balance Model (Held et al., 2010; Geoffroy et al., 2013).

Core Coupled Governing Equations

C_m · (dΔT_s / dt) = ΔF - λ·ΔT_s - γ·(ΔT_s - ΔT_d)
C_d · (dΔT_d / dt) = γ·(ΔT_s - ΔT_d)

Calibrated Physical Parameters

  • Radiative Forcing Benchmark ($F_{2\times}$): $3.71\text{ W/m}^2$ per doubling of atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$. Calculated via $\Delta F = 5.35 \cdot \ln(C / 280) + \Delta F_{\text{feedbacks}}$.
  • Climate Feedback Parameter ($\lambda$): Set to $1.236\text{ W/m}^2/\text{K}$, establishing an Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS) of exactly $\text{ECS} = \frac{3.71}{1.236} = 3.00^\circ\text{C}$ (matching the IPCC AR6 central consensus estimate).
  • Mixed-Layer Heat Capacity ($C_m$): $8.2\text{ W}\cdot\text{yr/m}^2/\text{K}$ (equivalent to a $\approx 75\text{ m}$ global ocean mixed layer).
  • Deep-Ocean Heat Capacity ($C_d$): $105.0\text{ W}\cdot\text{yr/m}^2/\text{K}$ (diffusive thermal reservoir).
  • Ocean Heat Uptake Efficacy ($\gamma$): $0.73\text{ W/m}^2/\text{K}$, yielding a Transient Climate Response (TCR) of $\approx 1.80^\circ\text{C}$ per doubling.

Dynamic Effective Radiating Altitude Derivation

Rather than using an uncalibrated optical formula, the radiating layer altitude ($z_{\text{eff}}$) is derived from the atmospheric lapse rate ($\Gamma = 6.5\text{ K/km}$) and the Stefan-Boltzmann relation for outgoing thermal flux:

T_eff = (OLR / σ)^(1/4)
z_eff = (T_surface - T_eff) / Γ

This prevents geometric runaway artifacts: as $\text{CO}_2$ increases, $z_{\text{eff}}$ rises into colder air, suppressing $\text{OLR}$. As the surface and troposphere heat up, $T_{\text{surface}}$ and $\text{OLR}$ increase together until equilibrium is re-established.
